Who can get into the console

/roles

CareerPilot has six permissions. They are assigned by role, and SUPER_ADMIN can mint a custom role with any combination of them — so access is granted per person, not per job title.

PermissionWhat it unlocks
manage_passportEverything below — config, pricing, pathways, missions, assessment, companies, news, coins
manage_passport_categoriesAdd, rename, reweight, reorder or delete a scoring category. Separate from the above on purpose: a weight change rewrites how every member's score is computed, so it can be withheld from someone who may still write questions.
view_passport_membersRead-only member access. Enough for a support desk.
view_passport_funnelThe drop-off funnel and its CSV export. Separate because it hands out member phone numbers and emails in bulk for outreach — a telecaller needs this and nothing else. Every export is logged with who took it.
convert_passport_memberGrant a ₹1,599 membership without payment. Hand out sparingly.
use_passportThe member-facing product. Every student has this.

Today SUPER_ADMIN and TENANT_ADMIN hold all six, STAFF holds view-only, INSTRUCTOR holds none. To give one person more, create a role at /roles, tick the permissions, assign it. The permission list is served live from the code, so anything added later appears there by itself.

Never do this A CareerPilot member is not a separate record — it is an ordinary user with an embedded passport field, and staff who opened CareerPilot picked one up too. Deleting "all CareerPilot users" would delete your only TENANT_ADMIN, an instructor and two staff. To reset the product, clear the passport field; never delete the accounts.

Plan & entitlements

/admin/passport/config

Decides what CareerPilot costs and which parts of it are free. This is the commercial control panel, and it is data rather than code, so pricing changes never wait for a deployment.

Controls

Membership price
Currently ₹1,599. Every paywall, upgrade prompt and Razorpay order reads this one number. Change it here and the whole product follows within a page refresh.
Membership months
12. Sets the expiry stamped on a member the moment their payment clears.
Entitlements (11 rows)
One row per feature, each set to free or paid. This is the paywall. Moving a row to free opens it to everyone immediately, including the members who never paid.
Onboarding fields
What a new member is asked for at signup beyond name, mobile and email. Extra fields appear on the join form automatically.
Enabled
The master switch. Turning it off closes signup and shows "not available right now" to visitors.

Why the free three are free The assessment costs nothing to run — it is deterministic, with no AI call — and it produces the score that makes the ₹1,599 ask feel earned. The 7-day preview shows the shape of the plan so the paywall lands where the value is already obvious. Moving any of the paid rows to free gives the product away to all 138 students at once.
02

Assessment builder

/admin/passport/assessment

The question bank behind the free assessment. Since the assessment is the top of the funnel, whoever owns conversion needs to be able to change it without an engineer.

Controls

Questions
Add, edit, reorder, remove. Options with one marked correct.
Stage segments
Questions are grouped by the member's stage, so a first-year and a job-switcher are not asked the same things.
Scoring categories
Full CRUD, at the top of the screen. Six today: Career Clarity, Aptitude, Logical Reasoning, Technical Foundation, Communication, Employability. Each question feeds one, and the member sees a per-category breakdown. See below.
Reset
Restores the shipped default bank. Destroys your edits.

The category manager

Sits at the top of the screen. Add, rename, reweight, reorder and delete the categories every member is measured against.

Label
What the member reads. Change it whenever you like — it is not an identifier.
Key
Permanent once saved, and shown read-only. It is stored on every question, every mission pool and every pathway's focus list, so renaming it would not rename those — it would orphan all three. For a new category it is generated from the label.
Weight
0.1 to 3. How much this category moves the career score. Technical sits at 1.5 and Career Clarity at 1.0 because technical ability predicts more. Changing a weight re-computes every future score.
Order
The order the member reads their breakdown in. Use the ↑ ↓ buttons.
Remove
Blocked while anything still points at the category. Each row shows its counts — questions, missions, and pathways focusing on it — so you can see what to move first.
A new category needs three things, not one Creating the row is step one. It also needs questions tagged to it, or it is never scored, and a mission pool (Missions screen), or the day generator silently falls back to Career Clarity. The student product needs no change — an unknown category renders with its prettified label and a plain bullet.
This carries its own permission manage_passport_categories, separate from manage_passport. Someone can be trusted to write questions without being able to rewrite how every member's score is computed.
Already handled for you A member is never scored on a category they were not asked about — an unasked category is absent from their breakdown rather than showing as zero. So you can safely add a category before you have written questions for it.

04

Pathways

/admin/passport/pathways

The career tracks a member is sorted into by their assessment result — software_dev, it_bridge and any others you define. A pathway determines which roadmap and missions the member receives.

Full create, edit and delete. A member's pathway can also be corrected by hand from the Members screen.

Before deleting one Check whether anyone is on it. A member pointing at a pathway that no longer exists has no roadmap to follow.
05

Missions

/admin/passport/missions

The daily tasks behind Mission Control. A yearly membership dies without a reason to open the app on a Tuesday, and this is where that reason is written.

How the day works

  1. You author mission pools here, per pathway and per day.
  2. A member opening the dashboard gets today's missions drawn from the pool that matches them.
  3. Completing one credits XP, coins, and advances the streak.
There is no nightly job The day is derived from the timestamps on the member's own activity, not from a cron. Nothing has to run at midnight, and nothing can fail to run. A day cannot be "missed" by the system — only by the member.
The day rolls at 05:30 IST, not midnight The boundary is computed in UTC. A student practising at 1am IST is counted against the previous day, and someone active only between midnight and 05:30 will see their streak break. Worth fixing before you promote streaks heavily.

Members

/admin/passport/students

One person's entire CareerPilot history on one screen. This is the support desk: when someone rings up confused, everything you need is here.

Controls

Search
By name, mobile or email.
Add member
Create someone by hand — for a walk-in or a phone signup. They can then log in by OTP.
Edit
Fix a name, email, pathway or stage.
Activate / Deactivate
Deactivating blocks login with a readable message and is fully reversible. Prefer it to deletion in every case.
Delete
Removes the member. Their submissions, interviews and ledger lines do not follow them.
View answers
Their assessment responses, question by question.
View interviews
Every mock interview transcript and the stored feedback.
Watch for duplicate accounts Three people currently hold two accounts each on the same mobile number, with progress split between them — in each case the older record holds the paid membership and the newer one holds a fresh assessment. The cause is phone numbers stored inconsistently (+919876500001 vs 9876500001), so the duplicate check misses. Search by the last 10 digits before creating anyone.

Drop-off funnel

/admin/passport/funnel

Where every member stopped, and who to contact about it. Built to be worked from: open a stage, get a calling list.

How it is ordered

Stages are ranked by how close the person came to paying, not by how many are in them. Someone who opened the payment sheet and walked away is a warmer call than someone who never finished their OTP, even though there are far more of the latter. Within a stage the coldest lead is first — a newest-first list means the same fresh names get worked every morning and month-old leads never get called at all.

StageWhat happenedWhat to say
Opened checkout, did not payReached Razorpay and stopped. They had their card out.Call the same day. Price, or the payment failed?
Got their score, never boughtFinished the free assessment and saw the result.Lead with their score and what the paid roadmap fixes.
Verified, never startedOwns the account, has not taken the assessment.It is free and takes 10 minutes.
Never finished signing upFilled the form, never entered the OTP.Check the number is real — often a typo, or WhatsApp never arrived.
Paid, then went quietPaying member, nothing for 14+ days. Churn forming.Re-engage before renewal.
Membership expiredPaid once, the year ran out.Renewal offer with last year’s achievements.
Paid and activeNothing to do. Present so the numbers add up.Leave them alone.

Controls

Open a stage
Reveals the people in it with mobile, email, days stuck and career score.
WhatsApp / Email
One tap per person. The WhatsApp link adds the country code for you.
Export CSV
The same list as a calling sheet. Logged with who exported it — a file of member phone numbers should never leave anonymously.
Two honest limits Members who joined before this shipped have no verification stamp, so they are placed by what they did rather than when they verified. And there is no "started the assessment but did not finish" bucket: the assessment is only saved on submit, so a half-finished one leaves no trace and the number would always be zero. Both are stated on the screen rather than hidden behind a plausible figure.

Companies — the deepest screen

/admin/passport/companies

The content engine behind Prepare Interviews. Students do not prepare "for interviews", they prepare for TCS — and this is where a company becomes something worth opening. Nine tabs.

🏢 RosterCompaniesCompany profile Interview patternQuestions✨ Import / Generate ReviewInterview reportsRounds & Categories

What each tab does

🏢 Roster
The control board. Every company with a live/total counter, a readiness percentage, exactly which checks are outstanding, bulk paste to create many at once, ✨ Draft all missing, and the green ✓ Verify eligibility button.
Companies
The plain list — create, edit and delete individual companies, set type (product / service / startup), logo and website.
Company profile
The overview a student reads first: about, roles hired, location, industry, salary bands, and your tips.
Interview pattern
The rounds in the order a candidate faces them — name, duration, what it tests, the clearing bar, one tip each. Two or more rounds is what readiness requires.
Questions
The bank. Edit, publish, unpublish, delete. Only published questions count toward readiness.
✨ Import / Generate
Paste a batch of questions to parse and import, or have AI predict likely questions for a chosen round.
Review
Student-submitted questions awaiting approval. Approving pays the contributor 50 coins.
Interview reports
Interview experiences submitted by students. Approving pays 300 coins.
Rounds & Categories
The vocabulary — round types, question categories, difficulty labels, company types. These are editable rows, not fixed lists.

Taking a company live

There is no publish button. A company becomes visible the moment it clears four checks, and vanishes again if one stops being true.

  1. Overview written — the profile tab, or let AI draft it.
  2. Two or more rounds on the interview pattern.
  3. Twenty published questions. Drafts do not count.
  4. Eligibility verified by a human — the green tick on the roster row.

The roster row tells you which of the four is still missing. Readiness is computed every time it is asked for and never stored, so it can never drift out of step with the content behind it.

The eligibility tick is deliberately manual AI drafts the CGPA, backlog and gap rules; a person confirms them. A student plans their year around those numbers, so this is the one step that is not automated. Read what was drafted before you tick it.
Renaming is safe Editing a company's name does not change its URL. Existing links keep working. If you paste a numbered list, the numbering is stripped from both the name and the URL.

Today: 21 companies on the roster, 1 live (Cognizant). Most of the rest are two checks away — questions, and the eligibility tick.

Tech news

/admin/passport/news

A short daily feed. A cheap reason for a member to open the app on a day when nothing else pulls them in. Publishing has to take a minute or it will not happen daily, which is why it is built around a paste box.

  1. Paste a public article URL.
  2. AI drafts a title and summary.
  3. Edit it — the draft is a starting point, not the finished item.
  4. Publish. It appears in the member feed immediately. Unpublishing removes it just as fast.
Guarded The fetcher refuses internal and private addresses, so the server cannot be talked into fetching its own network on request.

Today: 1 item published. This needs a publishing rhythm, not more software.

Coins

/admin/passport/coins

The motivation economy. Two sections — Economy settings and Earning rules — plus the ledger. Every number is editable, so the economy can be tuned against real behaviour instead of guesses.

Economy settings

Coins per rupee
250 — the exchange rate for anything redeemable.
Monthly earn cap
5,000 coins. The brake on the whole economy.
Annual cost budget
₹100 per member per year of real reward cost.
Expiry
12 months.
Minimum redemption
100 coins.
Free members accrue
On — unpaid members still build a balance, which gives them something to lose by not upgrading.

Earning rules — each with its own daily cap

Read this before you test coins Six rules are set to zero, so most actions credit nothing and the feature looks broken when it is merely unconfigured. A member's realistic maximum today is 12 coins a day. Set real amounts before demoing this.

Separately, coins can be earned but not spent — there is no redemption catalogue, no coupons and no referral flow yet. Until there is, the economy teaches members that coins are meaningless.

The ledger

Every credit, with its reason, its member and its time. Each carries an idempotency key, so a replayed request can never double-credit. This is your audit trail — read it rather than trusting a balance.

Staging board

/admin/careerpilot/staging

Moves a member between career stages. Stage drives which missions and content they see, so when someone's situation changes — graduated, started job hunting, switched track — this corrects it without touching the database.
